Four ways the review surface could work

These are four genuinely different shapes for the same job — “here are the things that finished; say yes or no” — not four coats of paint on the row you have now. Each one is filled with the real work sitting on your board right now: the six design-core cards, the two cards on sprint/card-32, #29 and #31 on their own, and #30 which you already approved and is merging.

Pick one, or pick pieces — “B's words with D's keyboard” is a legitimate answer.

Today
What is actually wrong with it
  1. The row is titled by machine. Two card titles get glued together with a “+”, then “· 2 cards” is appended, and the result — “Cards show a live phase, not + Approved cards leave Needs you · 2 cards” — is not a sentence anyone would say.
  2. The row shows no evidence at all. Grouped rows only lead with a claim when every member shares one packet. Two cards written up separately on one branch — the normal case — fall through to card numbers and a branch name. The rows carrying the most work show the least about it.
  3. Two controls, one job. “Reviewing 1 of 2 →” is a status wearing a button's clothes, and “Review these 2” sits inside the row doing nearly the same thing. Nothing says how they relate.
  4. Numbers lead. 33 · 2 · 2 · 2 are the loudest things in the column, and none of them is what the work is.
  5. It is a table of rows about work, when what you wanted was to be handed the decisions.

A · One at a time

What this is: no list. The surface is the decision in front of you — the claim at reading size, what it covers, the checks, the picture, Approve / Send back. The rest of the queue is four quiet lines with no buttons.

Trades: you lose the overview entirely — no cherry-picking, no comparing two branches, and thirteen waiting is thirteen screens.

B · The digest

my pick

What this is: a briefing. Every finished branch is a short paragraph in plain English with the verdict as a verb at the end of it. Card numbers, branch, test counts all move to a small last line — nothing leads with a number.

Trades: longest of the four vertically; only works if the summary is genuinely well written, so a lazy paragraph is worse than a good table; poor for finding one specific card.

C · Contact sheet

What this is: the screenshot is the row. A grid of evidence tiles you judge by looking; the words are captions. A work unit is a bracket drawn around the tiles that shipped together, not a title glued out of titles.

Trades: favours work that photographs well — a server fix with three checks and no picture is a text tile in a room full of images — and a screenshot can look right while the thing behind it is wrong.

D · Inbox

What this is: mail. Sender is the agent, subject is the claim, snippet is the first check; the row you are on opens in place with the evidence and the buttons. j/k/a/b clears a queue without the mouse; a shared branch is a thread.

Trades: closest of the four to the list of database rows you already dislike — one line truncates the nuance — and multi-select approve edges toward the blind bulk approve the spec rules out.

If it were mine

B at rest, A when you decide. The list is the digest — paragraphs you can read straight down, no counts leading anything — and “Review next” opens exactly the one-at-a-time surface in A. That gets you the overview and the undivided decision without the two controls fighting, because they become one thing: the list is for reading, the panel is for deciding.

C is the strongest single answer if most of what you approve is UI, and it is the shape that Chaos costumes best. D is the right answer only if the queue is routinely ten or more deep — today it is four, and dense-and-fast is solving a problem you do not have.

All four run on the board's own Calm tokens and vendored fonts — no external requests, no CDN. Every claim, check step, test count, card number and branch name on these pages is real, lifted from the evidence packets on the live board; the screenshots are the real attachments, downscaled. Nothing here is wired to anything: the buttons do not post verdicts. Chaos translation is noted at the bottom of each option page.
