What this is / what it trades One decision fills the screen, at the size of something you are meant to read; the rest of the queue is four quiet lines with no buttons on them. Trades: you lose the overview — you cannot see all four packets at once, cherry-pick, or compare two branches, and a queue of thirteen is thirteen screens. Approving is easy; noticing that two of the four touch the same file is not.

How it held up in Chaos — survives One decision on screen is one dialogue box, and a dialogue box is the most native object in this whole vocabulary — the panel takes the 2px ink outline, the bevel and the signoff rail without arguing. The headline is the only place the pixel face is spent and it is spent at 15px, the product's own --size-claim: at that size a 40-character sentence is two lines in a 34ch measure, which is the most Press Start 2P can carry and still be read rather than decoded. Everything below it — the claim, the check steps, After this — stays in the body face, which is exactly what the product does. Nothing was resized to fit and nothing was reconstructed. What it cost: at 980 the headline steps to 13px, and the quiet After-this rows are thinner against purple than against Calm's near-black, because Calm's grey for a merged card lands under 3:1 here and had to be re-cut from the lilac ramp.
